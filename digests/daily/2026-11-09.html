Efficient Generative Retrieval for E-commerce Search with Semantic Cluster IDs and Expert-Guided RL

3 Nov · 7 citations

Generative retrieval promises to unify multi-stage retrieval into a single model, but its industrial e-commerce adoption is hindered by massive dynamic catalogs, strict latency requirements, and alignment with ranking goals. To address this, the authors propose a recall-stage supplement framework rather than an end-to-end replacement. Their method, CQ-SID, uses category-aware and query-item contrastive learning with Residual Quantized VAEs to encode items into hierarchical semantic cluster IDs. Additionally, they develop EG-GRPO, an expert-guided reinforcement learning approach that aligns generative recall with downstream ranking under sparse rewards. Offline experiments on TmallAPP search logs demonstrate significant improvements in click and exposure metrics while reducing inference time.

Key ideas
1 Position generative retrieval as a recall-stage supplement rather than an end-to-end replacement to better handle massive and dynamic e-commerce catalogs under strict latency requirements.
2 Use category-aware and query-item contrastive learning combined with Residual Quantized VAEs to encode items into hierarchical semantic cluster identifiers (CQ-SID).
3 Develop Expert-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (EG-GRPO), a reinforcement learning approach that aligns generative recall with downstream ranking under sparse rewards by injecting ground-truth samples.
📊 Results
Offline experiments on TmallAPP search logs show that CQ-SID achieves up to 26 percent gains in specific metrics, improving click and exposure while reducing beam search size and inference time.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Explore CQ-SID using hierarchical semantic cluster IDs to improve the recall stage of THE ICONIC's fashion search engine.
  • Adopt EG-GRPO reinforcement learning techniques to better align generative retrieval recall stages with downstream ranking goals like conversion rate or revenue.

SAM-D2Q: Aligning Multimodal Doc2Query with Search Demand and Conversion for E-commerce

3 Nov

E-commerce search frequently struggles with vocabulary mismatch between user queries and merchant product titles. Traditional text-only Doc2Query methods alleviate this but often fail to capture visual attributes and do not align with commercial objectives. To address this, the authors propose SAM-D2Q, a multimodal document expansion framework designed for e-commerce search under Boolean retrieval constraints. The method features a three-stage approach: task-adapted multimodal fine-tuning, multimodal data augmentation, and reinforcement-learning-based preference alignment targeting search business objectives. This ensures generated pseudo-queries match both user intent and commercial value.

Key ideas
1 Use a multimodal Doc2Query framework (SAM-D2Q) to bridge the vocabulary mismatch between user search queries and short merchant-authored product titles.
2 Incorporate product images alongside text during document expansion to capture visual attributes missed by text-only models.
3 Apply task-adapted multimodal supervised fine-tuning to enhance the vision-language understanding of product titles, images, and queries.
4 Use reinforcement-learning-based preference alignment to optimize generated pseudo-queries directly toward search business objectives and user intent.

Mine and Refine: Optimizing Graded Relevance in E-commerce Semantic Search Retrieval

3 Nov · 2 citations

Large-scale e-commerce embedding-based retrieval struggles with graded relevance, false negatives in hard sample mining, and unstable similarity score separability across relevance levels. To solve this, the authors propose Mine and Refine, a two-stage contrastive training framework utilizing a fine-tuned lightweight LLM as a guideline-aligned scalable labeler. Stage 1 builds a global embedding space using label-aware supervised contrastive learning. Stage 2 mines hard samples, re-annotates them to remove spurious negatives, and refines embeddings using a multi-level extension of circle loss for margin-controlled separation. Deployed in production e-commerce search, the approach successfully improves user engagement and gross order value.

Key ideas
1 Use a fine-tuned lightweight LLM with engagement-driven audit as a scalable, guideline-aligned relevance labeler throughout training.
2 Implement a two-stage contrastive training framework where Stage 1 establishes a robust global embedding space via label-aware supervised contrastive learning.
3 Address false negatives in hard sample mining by re-annotating mined hard samples using the LLM labeler.
4 Refine embedding models using a multi-level extension of circle loss to enforce margin-controlled separation across different relevance levels.
📊 Results
Deployed in production e-commerce search across multiple product verticals, the approach delivers statistically significant lifts in user engagement and gross order value.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Adopt the two-stage Mine and Refine contrastive training framework to improve semantic search retrieval and hybrid search score fusion for fashion catalog queries.
  • Use a fine-tuned lightweight LLM as an automated relevance labeler to handle graded relevance and business guidelines in search and recommendation datasets.
  • Apply multi-level extensions of circle loss to enforce better margin-controlled separation across different relevance levels for fashion product recommendations.

A Novel E-Commerce Search Engine Based on Multimodal Generative AI for Improving Product Accessibility

21 Oct · Prince Sultan University

Traditional e-commerce search engines struggle with accuracy and fairness due to heavy reliance on keyword matching and systemic biases. This paper introduces a novel search architecture that combines Large Language Models for semantic query understanding with Multimodal LLMs to extract rich visual attributes from product images. The authors fine-tune LLaVA on e-commerce data to generate enhanced product descriptions and employ a fine-tuned CLIP model to create unified embeddings for both images and text. These embeddings are stored in a vector database to enable efficient and relevant semantic search alongside integrated recommendations. The approach successfully addresses limitations in visual product interpretation and search relevance.

Key ideas
1 Use fine-tuned LLaVA to extract detailed visual attributes from product images and enrich text descriptions for e-commerce items.
2 Fine-tune CLIP to generate a unified embedding space for both product images and enriched text descriptions.
3 Store multimodal product embeddings in a vector database to power efficient semantic search and recommendation mechanisms.
4 Leverage Large Language Models for advanced semantic query understanding to overcome the limitations of traditional keyword matching.
