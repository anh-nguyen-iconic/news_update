LLM-Driven Generative Fashion Outfit Algorithm Based on Consumer Intent Detection

2m ago · Communication University of China

This paper addresses the challenge of providing personalized aesthetic experiences in fashion recommendations, where traditional methods struggle with user intent and large language models (LLMs) lack fine-grained item understanding. To solve this, the authors propose a generative outfit recommendation algorithm driven by consumer intent detection. The approach encodes fashion items into hierarchical semantic IDs to help LLMs process large catalogs and uses chain-of-thought reasoning to extract interpretable intent from implicit user behaviors. This extracted intent is then used as a condition within a multi-task fine-tuning framework for end-to-end outfit generation. Experiments demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing baselines across hit rate, rank accuracy, diversity, and compatibility metrics.

Key ideas
1 Encode fashion items into hierarchical semantic IDs to bridge the gap in letting LLMs perceive large-scale item catalogs effectively.
2 Employ chain-of-thought reasoning to extract interpretable intent descriptions directly from implicit user behaviors.
3 Incorporate the extracted consumer intent as an explicit condition into a multi-task fine-tuning framework for coordinated outfit generation.

No quantitative results reported.

Perplexity and Intelligence

26h ago

Modern AI progress is primarily driven by training large predictors or compressors on vast datasets, with pretraining perplexity serving as a constant foundational metric for intelligence. OpenAI argues for establishing cross-entropy loss and perplexity as transparent shared metrics across model developers to compare progress and set safety standards. Tracking this metric shows that specific log-perplexity thresholds consistently unlock emergent capabilities like in-context learning, chain-of-thought reasoning, and advanced mathematics. As models approach lower losses and potentially accelerate deep learning research itself, the industry needs shared norms and limits based on these metrics. Ultimately, standardizing perplexity tracking can help coordinate safety evaluations before new transformative capabilities emerge.

Key ideas
1 Establish perplexity or cross-entropy loss on a held-out dataset as a shared industry metric to compare base model pretraining progress and inform safety standards.
2 Recognize that lower pretraining perplexity correlates with emergent latent capabilities, such as GPT-3.5 at ~0.99 log-perplexity enabling fluent conversation and GPT-4 at ~0.91 enabling chain-of-thought reasoning.
3 Anticipate further transformative capabilities and challenges as models enter the ~0.68-0.7 log-perplexity window.
4 Establish industry-wide common limits and shared norms for evaluating and red-teaming frontier models before crossing specific perplexity thresholds, until monitoring capabilities catch up.
📊 Results
GPT-3.5 reached a log-perplexity of approximately 0.99, GPT-4 achieved roughly 0.91, and newer models have reached losses below 0.8 with specific mathematical breakthroughs occurring at ~0.76 log-perplexity.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Track pretraining loss or perplexity internally to gauge foundational model quality when selecting open-source or commercial base models for tasks like search query understanding and product recommendations.
  • Use pretraining perplexity milestones as an indicator of when to test models for new complex reasoning capabilities relevant to customer personalisation or automated LLM agents.

Combatting adversarial distillation

20 Nov

Adversarial distillation allows bad actors to extract advanced capabilities and internal reasoning traces from frontier AI models without deploying comparable safety safeguards. OpenAI addresses this by combining request-level mitigations, classifiers, API controls, and campaign-level investigations to disrupt unauthorized extraction attempts. A key focus is protecting models' internal reasoning steps, known as chain-of-thought, which can transfer risky dual-use skills if exposed. Updated safeguards in models like GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol successfully reduced average attack success rates against protected reasoning. These multi-layered defenses help maintain model safety while supporting the secure proliferation of frontier AI.

Key ideas
1 Protect intermediate reasoning traces (chain-of-thought) to prevent the unauthorized transfer of advanced capabilities in dual-use domains like cybersecurity and biology.
2 Implement request-level defenses using input and output classifiers to block protected reasoning leaks in assistant messages and tool-call arguments.
3 Enforce API-level restrictions that tie a model's encrypted reasoning strictly to its original conversation history and prevent cross-model reuse.
4 Combine request-level technical controls with campaign-level monitoring, investigation, and network-wide enforcement against abusive accounts.
📊 Results
In an internal adaptive evaluation using automated red-teaming, the average attack success rate fell from 54.0% against GPT-6 Astra with original safeguards to 0.2% against GPT-6.2 Astra with updated safeguards.

Announcing Amazon Textract Insights: Verifiable generative AI for documents

20 Nov

Organizations often struggle to extract structured data from complex, mixed-layout document packets without building fragile custom pipelines or relying on ungrounded general-purpose LLMs. AWS is announcing Amazon Textract Insights, a generative AI feature that processes documents through a new API using natural language descriptions. Users define desired fields via a JSON Schema blueprint and submit mixed packets of up to 3,000 pages to be automatically split and matched. The service returns structured results where each value includes a confidence score and page-level grounding. This enables auditable automation and helps teams decide where to route low-confidence fields for manual review.

Key ideas
1 Define extraction requirements using a JSON Schema blueprint containing natural language descriptions and inference types for explicit or inferred fields.
2 Submit mixed multi-document packets up to 3,000 pages from Amazon S3 to automatically split files, match sub-documents to blueprints, and extract fields via a single asynchronous API call.
3 Receive confidence scores and page-level grounding (coordinates and page numbers) for every extracted value to enable auditable automation.
4 Use pricing structured per page rather than per token, with support for PDF, PNG, JPEG, and TIFF formats across multiple languages.

No quantitative results reported.

Training-Free Multimodal Fashion Recommendation with Expert-Knowledge Alignment via ResRAG

6 Nov · Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Multimodal fashion recommendation often struggles to align complex visual and textual information with domain-specific expert knowledge. This paper introduces ResRAG, a training-free framework that leverages Retrieval-Augmented Generation to align expert knowledge for fashion recommendation tasks. By avoiding the need for resource-intensive model retraining, the approach integrates external knowledge bases to improve recommendation relevance. The method successfully bridges the gap between multimodal item representations and professional fashion expertise without incurring heavy computational costs. Findings indicate that this training-free, knowledge-aligned approach enhances recommendation performance effectively.

Key ideas
1 Utilize a training-free Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework to inject domain-specific expert knowledge into fashion recommendation pipelines.
2 Align multimodal item representations (visual and textual) with external fashion knowledge bases without retraining core recommendation models.
3 Leverage expert-knowledge alignment to handle complex fashion compatibility and trend queries more accurately.
