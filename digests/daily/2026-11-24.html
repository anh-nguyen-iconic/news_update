Mine and Refine: Optimizing Graded Relevance in E-commerce Semantic Search Retrieval

6 Nov · 4 citations
Figure 1. Mine and Refine Overview: Stage 1 trains a shared encoder on the original dataset using supervised contrastive (SupCon) loss. Stage 2 continues training on a curriculum dataset using circle loss with self-pacing sample weighting…

Embedding-based retrieval in e-commerce struggles with graded relevance, noisy engagement signals, false negatives in hard sample mining, and unstable similarity score separability. To resolve these challenges, the authors propose 'Mine and Refine', a two-stage contrastive training framework that utilizes an engagement-audited fine-tuned LLM as a guideline-aligned scalable labeler. Stage 1 establishes a global embedding space using label-aware supervised contrastive learning, while Stage 2 mines hard samples, re-annotates them via the LLM, and refines the model using a multi-level extension of circle loss for margin-controlled separation across relevance levels. Deployed in production search across multiple verticals, the framework delivers statistically significant lifts in user engagement, conversion rates, and gross order value.

Key ideas
1 Fine-tune a lightweight LLM (GPT-4o mini) on human-annotated data audited with engagement signals to act as a guideline-aligned scalable labeler for graded relevance (labels 2, 1, 0).
2 Employ a two-stage training framework where Stage 1 uses label-aware supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) and Stage 2 incorporates offline mined hard samples.
3 Extend circle loss to multi-level relevance labels with per-level margin targets to sharpen similarity boundaries and ensure cross-query score consistency for stable downstream hybrid fusion.
4 Use an additive spelling augmentation strategy that preserves original queries while introducing probabilistic typo variants, avoiding the degradation caused by invariance regularization.
5 Enrich training data with catalog attributes (concatenated item name and category path) and selectively add synthetic queries only for items lacking positive query-item pairs.
📊 Results
Offline evaluation on 155M query-item pairs showed superior performance compared to pretrained models up to 6x larger. Online A/B tests in production yielded statistically significant lifts in add-to-cart rate, conversion rate, and gross order value.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Fine-tune a small open-source or commercial LLM using THE ICONIC's historical search logs and human relevance guidelines to automatically label search query-product pairs into graded relevance levels (e.g., exact match, substitute, irrelevant).
  • Adopt the multi-level circle loss and two-stage Mine and Refine training pipeline to improve semantic product search and product recommendation retrieval, ensuring better score separability for downstream ranking models.
  • Implement additive spelling augmentation using character-level noise models to improve search retrieval robustness against customer typos and misspellings without degrading performance on clean queries.

MOON3.0: Reasoning-aware Multimodal Representation Learning for E-commerce Product Understanding

9 Nov · 4 citations
Figure 3. Pipeline of our MOON3.0. Each element in the training triplet (query, positive, and negative items) is represented in three modalities: image-only, text-only, and multimodal (image and text). “Attr.” denotes product attributes.

Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for e-commerce product representation often act as passive feature extractors that encode global embeddings, ignoring fine-grained attributes like collar style or material and suffering from attention dilution, imitation bottlenecks, and feature attenuation. To solve this, the authors propose MOON3.0, a reasoning-aware MLLM framework that explicitly deconstructs multimodal product inputs into structured, multi-dimensional attributes via autoregressive chain-of-thought reasoning before embedding generation. The architecture integrates a multi-head modality fusion module, a joint contrastive and reinforcement learning framework optimized with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and multi-dimensional rewards, and a fine-grained residual enhancement (FIRE) module to preserve local details. Additionally, the authors release a large-scale multimodal e-commerce benchmark named MBE3.0 containing nearly 7.7 million training samples with reasoning supervision. Experimental evaluations show that MOON3.0 achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across diverse multimodal retrieval, classification, and attribute prediction tasks on…

Key ideas
1 Adopt a 'think-then-embed' paradigm where the MLLM first generates a structured multi-dimensional rationale (attribute key-value pairs) autoregressively before extracting the final embedding from the last token hidden state.
2 Use a multi-head modality fusion module that computes dynamic consistency gates and head-specific subspace gating weights to integrate complementary raw image and text signals into the base reasoning representation without dilution.
3 Implement a joint contrastive and reinforcement learning framework optimizing via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using a composite reward function spanning format compliance, token length limits, log-rank retrieval accuracy, and MLLM-verified attribute quality.
4 Deploy a Fine-Grained Residual Enhancement (FIRE) module featuring patch-level visual gating, intermediate multi-scale visual layer injection into early LLM layers, and long-range language decoding residual connections to prevent local detail attenuation.
📊 Results
Evaluated on the newly introduced MBE3.0 benchmark (which features 7,691,199 training samples with reasoning supervision and 916,188 test samples) and public datasets like M5Product and Fashion200K, MOON3.0 achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across retrieval, classification, and attribute prediction tasks using a compact 256-dimensional embedding space.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Upgrade product search and multimodal retrieval systems by implementing the reasoning-aware 'think-then-embed' architecture, allowing queries containing complex attributes (e.g., 'red sweater with lace collar and bowknot') to match accurately against catalogue items.
  • Improve attribute extraction and catalogue quality pipelines by utilizing fine-grained attribute deconstruction policies inspired by MOON3.0's structured rationale generation to automatically tag and classify fashion items.
  • Enhance product recommendations and personalized tile generation by deploying a multi-head modality fusion and fine-grained residual enhancement (FIRE) module to better capture nuanced visual and textual details of fashion apparel.

Efficient Generative Retrieval for E-commerce Search with Semantic Cluster IDs and Expert-Guided RL

6 Nov · 9 citations
Figure 1. Architecture overview of our generative recall framework, showing the pipeline: (1) CQ-SID item encoding, (2) progressive query-to-SID learning, and (3) EG-GRPO ranking alignment.

Industrial e-commerce search engines face severe scalability, latency, and ranking alignment challenges when deploying generative retrieval models. This paper proposes a recall-stage generative retrieval framework featuring CQ-SID, which encodes items into hierarchical semantic cluster IDs using category-guided residual quantization and query-item contrastive learning. To optimize query-to-SID mapping, the authors employ a progressive four-stage training pipeline built on a lightweight Qwen2.5-0.5B model. Additionally, they introduce EG-GRPO, an expert-guided reinforcement learning technique that stabilizes training under sparse rewards by injecting ground-truth samples to align retrieval with downstream ranking. Offline experiments and online A/B tests demonstrate significant gains in hitrates, reduced beam search sizes, and a 1.15% increase in GMV in production.

Key ideas
1 CQ-SID replaces unique item IDs with cluster semantic identifiers using category-guided residual quantization (RQ-VAE) and bidirectional query-item contrastive learning, balancing aggregation and inter-cluster discriminability.
2 A post-processing random grouping strategy splits overly large SID clusters based on a maximum threshold to prevent popular items from dominating beam search and causing retrieval collapse.
3 A progressive four-stage training pipeline transitions from item-to-SID mapping to query-to-SID, personalized user-augmented UQ2SID, and finally ranking-aligned refinement.
4 EG-GRPO (Expert-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization) injects ground-truth samples as pseudo-generated outputs into the policy gradient group to stabilize reinforcement learning in sparse-reward settings.
📊 Results
Offline experiments on TmallAPP search logs show CQ-SID achieves up to 26.76% relative gain in semantic click hitrate over RQ-VAE baselines. Online A/B tests confirm improvements in GMV (+1.15%) and UCTCVR (+0.40%), with the generative channel accounting for 50.25% of exposures, 58.96% of clicks, and 72.63% of purchases.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Adopt CQ-SID cluster semantic identifiers for THE ICONIC's search recall stage to replace or supplement traditional dense retrieval, grouping fashion items by category hierarchies to reduce beam search size and inference latency.
  • Implement the progressive query-to-SID training pipeline (leveraging a small language model like Qwen2.5-0.5B) incorporating user profiling and recent interaction histories to improve personalized fashion search recommendations.
  • Utilize EG-GRPO to align recall candidate generation with downstream ranking and conversion objectives, using expert sample injection to handle sparse click and purchase rewards in fashion e-commerce.

How to build and maintain an agentic evaluation

34h ago

Building and maintaining agentic AI evaluations requires defining domain-specific tasks, robust scoring mechanisms, and transparent infrastructure to allow meaningful external scrutiny. The article outlines an eight-step guide for creating an agentic evaluation, moving from initial claims and task adaptation to validation against shortcuts and multi-model benchmarking. Using an open-source evaluation harness (Harbor) and a physics lab use case (fitting Raman spectra), the authors demonstrate how hidden flaws like early completion or missing package dependencies affect reliability. Ultimately, strategic open-sourcing of environments, verifiers, and traces prevents benchmark obsolescence and enables continuous improvement.

Key ideas
1 Test evaluation robustness against a lazy shortcut or a do-nothing agent to ensure the verifier rejects answers that skip required reasoning or skills.
2 Structure metrics using both pass@k (at least one success) and pass^k (consistent success) to distinguish between human-reviewed workflows and unattended processing.
3 Adopt a strategic open-sourcing model by publishing environments, harnesses, and traces on platforms like the Hugging Face Hub while protecting active test data against contamination.
4 Evaluate agent traces rather than final scores alone to diagnose specific failure modes like early task completion or missing environment dependencies.
📊 Results
On the eight adapted raman-lab tasks across three rollouts per task, the initial setup passed 11 of 24 rollouts with a pass@3 of 7/8 and a pass^3 of 1/8, yielding a mean pass rate of 0.46 with a standard error of 0.11.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Build agentic evaluations for complex search query understanding or multi-step recommendation workflows by adapting open-source harnesses and pinning specific inference providers.
  • Implement rigorous verifier checks and shortcut testing on automated product attribute extraction tasks to ensure LLMs perform the actual extraction rather than hallucinating plausible values.
  • Track agent traces (using shared formats like ATIF) for size & fit or LLM agent applications to diagnose whether failures stem from tool use, early stopping, or missing dependencies.

OneMall: One Architecture, More Scenarios — End-to-End Generative Recommender Family for E-commerce

6 Nov · 23 citations
Figure 5. The RL workflow of our OneMall.

Industrial e-commerce platforms like Kuaishou need to balance diverse item formats such as product-cards, short-videos, and live-streams under strict commercial objectives like Gross Merchandise Value (GMV). To address the limitations of traditional cascaded recommendation systems, the authors present OneMall, an end-to-end generative recommendation framework that adapts modern LLM pre-training and post-training paradigms to e-commerce. OneMall unifies multi-scenario item tokenization using business-aware contrastive fine-tuning and FSQ quantization, employs a pure Transformer backbone with Query-Formers and Sparse Mixture-of-Experts for scalable auto-regressive generation, and applies reinforcement learning (DPO/GRPO) to use an online ranking model as a reward signal. Extensive offline and online evaluations show that OneMall achieves consistent and substantial GMV lifts across all deployed services while serving over 400 million daily active users.

Key ideas
1 E-commerce Semantic Tokenizer: Fine-tunes an LLM using Swin-Transformer and Qwen2.5 with a contrastive objective on commercial and watching Item2Item corpora, combined with Res-Kmeans and FSQ for multi-level Semantic ID generation.
2 Query-Former Sequence Compression: Compresses long, multi-aspect historical user behavior sequences (exposure, click, buy) into compact latent tokens to maintain transformer computational efficiency.
3 Decoder-Style Sparse MoE Transformer: Uses causal self-attention and loss-free sparse Mixture-of-Experts to auto-regressively generate multi-level Semantic IDs while scaling model capacity for online serving.
4 Ranking-Supervised Reinforcement Learning: Connects the retrieval and ranking models using DPO and GRPO, taking the online ranking model as a reward function to optimize end-to-end policy retrieval.
📊 Results
OneMall achieved online A/B test GMV lifts of +14.7% in Product-Card, +10.3% in Short-Video, and +4.9% in Live-Streaming scenarios at Kuaishou, serving over 400 million daily active users.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Adopt the multi-level Semantic ID tokenizer approach using FSQ and Res-Kmeans to unify product-card and video/content recommendations across THE ICONIC's app and site.
  • Implement Query-Former sequence compression to efficiently feed long user interaction histories (clicks, purchases, cart additions) into recommendation models without blowing up inference latency.
  • Explore connecting candidate generation (retrieval) and ranking models using GRPO reinforcement learning where THE ICONIC's conversion/revenue ranking model acts as the reward signal.
