Co-Designing Sparse Attention for Efficient Long-Context Inference

8h ago

As context lengths grow to hundreds of thousands of tokens, dense attention becomes computationally expensive, leading transformer designs to adopt sparse attention. While sparse attention reduces core attention costs by letting queries attend to only a subset of keys, the indexer that selects those keys still must score every key in the context. This NVIDIA technical blog provides a practical primer on co-designing hardware-efficient sparse attention models for NVIDIA GPUs. It evaluates design choices across indexer query heads, key heads, head dimensions, compression ratios, and speculative decoding depths. The findings offer eight concrete guidelines for optimizing prefill and decode performance across the indexer, Top-k, and core attention stages.

Key ideas
1 Keep the product of group size (G) and speculative draft length (D + 1) within one Tensor Core tile (about 200 rows, or 256 with an FP16 epilogue).
2 Use key heads (KH) equal to 1 with a cross-head reduction over query heads to write only one logit per key.
3 Choose a head dimension (Hsz) of 128 or 256 to avoid leaving Tensor Cores waiting on CUDA-core epilogues.
4 Prefer block-level selection with large blocks in core attention to enable bulk fetching and sharing across queries.
5 Treat Top-k as part of the indexer budget, fusing it with the indexer and reusing previous steps during decode.
📊 Results
No quantitative results reported.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Apply block-level selection and large group sizes when training or deploying custom LLMs for long-context customer service agents and automated fashion styling assistants.
  • Optimize inference latency for large catalog understanding and query understanding workloads by tuning indexer parameters like head dimensions and compression ratios based on NVIDIA GPU hardware constraints.

AN INTELLIGENT CONSUMER BEHAVIOUR PREDICTION SYSTEM USING HYBRID MACHINE LEARNING ALGORITHMS

25 Oct · Starex University

Predicting whether website visitors will make a purchase is challenging due to rare conversions and noisy behaviour. This paper proposes an intelligent consumer behaviour prediction system that combines unsupervised segmentation with a stacked ensemble of tree-based learners. The approach uses clustering to separate user segments with drastically different conversion rates, while a stacked ensemble of Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM models handles classification. Evaluated on the public Online Shoppers Purchasing Intention dataset, the hybrid model achieves high predictive performance. The findings demonstrate that stacking heterogeneous learners drives most of the accuracy gains, whereas the clustering step primarily enhances interpretability.

Key ideas
1 Combine unsupervised customer segmentation with supervised classification to separate high-conversion from low-conversion visitor segments.
2 Deploy a stacked ensemble architecture using Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM base learners with a logistic-regression meta-learner.
3 Engineer aggregate behavioural features from browsing sessions to improve purchase intent prediction accuracy.
4 Tune decision thresholds strictly on training data to optimize evaluation metrics like F1-score and recall on held-out test sets.
📊 Results
Evaluated on the Online Shoppers Purchasing Intention dataset of 12,330 sessions, the hybrid model reached an AUC of 0.929, an F1-score of 0.678, and a recall of 0.715. Unsupervised clustering successfully isolated user segments with a 29.4% conversion rate versus 1.8%.

Jointly Optimizing Debiased CTR and Uplift for Coupons Marketing: A Unified Causal Framework

3 Nov

Marketing interventions like coupons introduce confounding bias into Click-Through Rate prediction by mixing intrinsic user preferences with treatment-induced uplift, miscalibrating base CTRs and distorting ranking and billing. To solve this, the authors propose the Unified Multi-Valued Treatment Network, which disentangles confounding factors from treatment-sensitive representations. It uses a full-space counterfactual inference module to jointly reconstruct debiased base CTR and intensity-response curves for multi-valued treatments. An auxiliary intensity estimation task captures treatment propensities and normalizes intervention effects across a continuous coupon-value spectrum. The method simultaneously achieves debiased CTR prediction and optimal uplift modeling.

Key ideas
1 Disentangle confounding factors from treatment-sensitive representations to isolate intrinsic user preferences from marketing intervention effects.
2 Deploy a full-space counterfactual inference module to jointly reconstruct debiased base CTR and intensity-response curves.
3 Use an auxiliary intensity estimation task to capture treatment propensities for multi-valued treatments.
4 Devise a unit uplift objective that normalizes intervention effects for comparable estimation across a continuous coupon-value spectrum.

Real-World End-to-End Product Entity Resolution

21h ago

Product Entity Resolution (PER) in e-commerce faces challenges from unstructured text, informal aliases, fine-grained alphanumeric differences, and promotional noise. The authors present an end-to-end evaluation of four zero-shot PER pipelines—Lexical Cascade, Semantic Cascade, Hybrid Fusion Cascade, and Generative-Lexical Cascade—across established benchmarks alongside two newly introduced real-world datasets called RETAILERS. They evaluate lexical and dense blocking combined with cross-encoder, tensor search, and Large Language Model verification methods, analyzing accuracy, latency, and hardware memory consumption. The findings reveal that lexical and semantic retrieval exhibit complementary failure modes, with lexical blocking prioritizing precision and efficiency while semantic retrieval handles vocabulary variation at the cost of false positives. Ultimately, the choice of optimal pipeline depends heavily on dataset characteristics and operational constraints.

Key ideas
1 Evaluate four end-to-end PER pipelines (Lexical, Semantic, Hybrid Fusion, and Generative-Lexical) without relying on task-specific fine-tuning to avoid operational data-labeling bottlenecks.
2 Use Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) to combine discrete BM25 lexical blocking and continuous bi-encoder semantic search in a Hybrid Fusion architecture, eliminating individual failure modes without trained rankers.
3 Deploy a Generative-Lexical Cascade where an LLM acts offline to normalize raw datasets into canonical representations prior to blocking, enabling high-speed lexical retrieval without GPU inference at query time.
4 Perform empirical resource profiling to identify that host CPU RAM—driven by uncompressed HNSW spatial adjacency lists during dense blocking—acts as the primary scalability bottleneck rather than GPU VRAM.
📊 Results
On the WDC-Products benchmark, BM25 operates approximately 4.7 times faster than the bi-encoder with HNSW (512.35 seconds vs 2,391.84 seconds) and maintains a much smaller index footprint (0.19 GB index overhead, 4.23 GB total RAM versus 14.50 GB for HNSW). Furthermore, the hybrid GPT configuration achieves a peak F1-score of 0.666 on RETAILERS-D while executing roughly 34 times faster than its local Gemma counterpart.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Apply hybrid fusion retrieval (BM25 combined with bi-encoder dense search via Reciprocal Rank Fusion) to improve product matching and catalog deduplication across diverse vendor data feeds.
  • Utilize offline LLM-based canonicalization to normalize noisy product titles, brands, and attributes before indexing to enhance downstream search and recommendation recall without introducing query-time latency.
