MIPR: A Benchmark for Reasoning-Intensive Multimodal Intent-to-Product Retrieval with Multi-Category Solutions

21h ago

Real-world e-commerce queries often express vague user needs via multimodal inputs where a single intent can be fulfilled by products across multiple distinct categories. To address this, the authors introduce MIPR, a benchmark for Reasoning-Intensive Multimodal Intent-to-Product Retrieval featuring 3,522 queries, a shared corpus of 30,731 products, and fine-grained relevance labels. The benchmark is constructed using a pipeline combining query curation, candidate product-term generation, deduplication, and multi-model labeling. Experiments show that existing embedding retrievers struggle with multi-category solution coverage and fine-grained ranking, while rerankers yield notable improvements but still fall far short of the oracle upper bound.

Key ideas
1 Formulating intent-to-product retrieval with a five-way relevance schema (L1 optimal, L2 suboptimal, N1 constraint-violating, N2 related non-solution, and N3 irrelevant) to capture complex real-world e-commerce matching constraints.
2 Using a two-stage product-term generation and reflection process to discover plausible solution types spanning specialized, substitute, complementary, and prerequisite categories.
3 Constructing a shared product corpus via clustering and deduplication within leaf categories to maintain cross-query competition while managing annotation scale.
4 Employing pooling-based judgment expansion using multiple multimodal embedding models to increase evaluation coverage and uncover unjudged relevant items.
📊 Results
MIPR comprises 3,522 multimodal queries and 30,731 products with 447,809 judgments. The best embedding retriever achieves an nDCG@10 of 49.23%, while reranking with Qwen3-VL-Reranker-8B improves nDCG@10 to 60.11% and solution-category recall@10 to 67.00%.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Adopt the five-way relevance labeling schema and multi-category solution taxonomy to evaluate and improve search relevance for open-ended customer queries like outfit styling or problem-solving searches.
  • Implement a multi-route candidate generation strategy combining term-based, text embedding, and multimodal retrieval to enhance recall for complex lifestyle queries.
  • Explore the use of multimodal rerankers such as Qwen3-VL-Reranker to re-order top retrieval results based on fine-grained user constraints and visual context.

Building secure mobile apps in the agentic era

5h ago

Shopify faced a massive surge in code throughput due to agent-driven mobile app development, increasing code changes by 11x. To maintain security without slowing down development, they developed an automated system using AI agents to map attack surfaces, enforce security skills, and review changes daily. The approach breaks down complex codebases into manageable inventories, analyzing one component at a time for vulnerabilities. Consequently, this method allows the team to securely scale mobile app releases at an unprecedented pace.

Key ideas
1 Map the entire mobile attack surface by categorizing security-sensitive elements like deep links, WebViews, and stored data into clearly defined inventories.
2 Codify security best practices and past incident learnings into modular 'skills' that both human reviewers and AI agents can load.
3 Keep AI security reviews scoped down to small, discrete items (such as a single deep link route) rather than scanning the entire app at once.
4 Automate daily reviews of code changes and external security news against the app map to catch vulnerabilities outside the immediate repository diff.
📊 Results
During a 12-week rewrite of the Shopify app, code throughput increased to an average of ~434,000 lines of code changed per week, compared to ~39,000 lines per week the previous year, representing an 11x increase.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Adopt agent-driven security skills and automated daily mapping to review frequent code and model updates in recommendation, search, and personalization services.
  • Break down complex e-commerce microservices and frontend web/app components into isolated inventories to improve automated vulnerability detection by AI agents.

Thinking Harder Doesn't Lead to Better Choices: Reasoning Effort Increases the Confidence but Not the Correctness of LLM Shopping Agents

21h ago

This paper investigates whether increasing reasoning effort improves the product-choice decisions of LLM-based shopping agents and how well calibrated their self-reported confidence is. The authors benchmarked ten frontier LLMs across three product-choice datasets (WebShop, Amazon SQID, and Wayfair WANDS) using fixed candidate lists with low, medium, and high reasoning effort settings. They found that increasing reasoning effort does not significantly improve decision correctness, but it does significantly increase the models' stated confidence. Furthermore, the models' calibration is heavily model-dependent, and errors are strongly shared across models rather than being independent.

Key ideas
1 Isolate the decision-making part of agentic shopping from search and navigation by presenting models with a fixed and shuffled list of ten candidate products (one correct target and nine distractors).
2 Use provider-defined reasoning effort controls (low, medium, high) via OpenRouter to test if higher inference-time compute improves shopping accuracy or changes confidence calibration.
3 Evaluate model confidence using verbalized 0-10 integers rescaled to probabilities, measuring Expected Calibration Error (ECE), Brier scores, and AUROC.
4 Quantify cross-model error sharing and pairwise error correlation to show that frontier models tend to fail on the exact same tasks and choose the same incorrect products.
📊 Results
Across ten frontier models and three datasets (WebShop, SQID, and WANDS) involving 37,128 total responses, increasing reasoning effort raised stated confidence significantly for 9 out of 10 models in WebShop, 5 of 10 in SQID, and 6 of 10 in WANDS, while correctness improved significantly for only three models in WebShop and even fewer in the other datasets. The variance of the number of wrong models per task was 6 to 8 times higher than what an independent-models binomial baseline would predict…
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Avoid blindly relying on higher inference-time compute or 'thinking effort' settings for LLM-driven product recommendations or automated search query understanding, as it risks inflating agent overconfidence without improving actual choice correctness.
  • Treat model calibration as a core selection criterion rather than a tuning target when deploying LLM agents for customer-facing applications like product recommendation chat or size & fit advice.
  • Be cautious when building multi-agent validation loops or ensembles for catalogue quality and attribute extraction; because frontier LLMs exhibit high pairwise error correlation and fail on the same distractors, inter-model agreement provides weak evidence of correctness.

Build Training-Ready Physical AI Datasets with Two Prompts in NVIDIA Cosmos

9h ago

Building high-quality training datasets for physical AI is often costly, time-consuming, and reliant on manual processes. NVIDIA Cosmos Data Factory introduces an open platform of frontier models, blueprints, and composable skills to automate data curation, processing, and synthetic data generation. Using AI coding agents, developers can set up environments and run specific data generation blueprints using just two prompts. The platform enables automated data augmentation, rare event simulation, and defect generation across various use cases. Main findings show significant speedups and cost reductions compared to traditional real-world data collection and manual labeling.

Key ideas
1 Use NVIDIA Cosmos Data Factory's two-prompt orchestration framework to configure your environment with Prompt 1 and execute specific data generation blueprints with Prompt 2.
2 Leverage the Image Attribute Augmentation blueprint to generate controlled variations of product or person crops, automatically labeling them with attributes and search queries.
3 Apply the Video Data Augmentation blueprint to transform existing footage across varying weather, lighting, and environmental conditions while preserving scene structure and auto-labeling the data.
4 Utilize AnomalyGen and defect generation blueprints to create realistic synthetic anomalies or rare event videos, filling training gaps without waiting for real-world occurrences.
📊 Results
AV dataset generation produced ~130 edge-case clips per day at $2.66 per clip (45x cheaper than road collection), image attribute augmentation processed ~42,649 pairs per day at $0.023 per pair (10x cheaper than purchased data), and defect image generation dropped dataset assembly time from two months to one day (a 60x speedup).
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Use Image Attribute Augmentation to generate synthetic variations of fashion products with controlled attribute changes (e.g., color, clothing type, accessories) to improve catalogue quality and search re-identification.
  • Apply Video Data Augmentation or style transfer skills to transform existing model or product video footage across different lighting and environmental conditions for enhanced marketing and visual search training.
