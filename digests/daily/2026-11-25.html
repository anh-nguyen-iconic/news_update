Sequential Multimodal Evidence Optimization for Product Media Ranking in E-Commerce

6 Nov
Figure 2. Complete end-to-end SMEO Framework

On modern e-commerce product pages, customers consume ordered sequences of multimodal media before making a purchase decision. Existing ranking systems often optimize myopic engagement metrics like clicks or treat media items independently, failing to account for cooperative information accumulation and variable customer drop-off. To address this, the authors present Sequential Multimodal Evidence Optimization (SMEO), a two-stage framework that first learns a trajectory utility model from consumed prefixes using position-aware learning and depth balancing. It then trains an autoregressive pointer network policy using survival-weighted reward-to-go to front-load decision-relevant media early. Evaluated offline on large-scale e-commerce sessions, SMEO improves estimated conversion by 5.5% and reduces swipes-to-conversion by 15% compared to existing baselines.

Key ideas
1 Formulate product media ranking as a sequential multimodal evidence optimization problem where assets cooperatively influence purchase decisions under variable customer stopping depths.
2 Decouple trajectory utility learning from policy optimization, using a Position-Aware Learning (PAL) architecture and survival-based depth reweighting to mitigate position bias and variable-depth imbalance.
3 Train an autoregressive pointer network policy with survival-weighted reward-to-go (RTG) using a frozen utility model to prioritize high-value media before likely customer drop-off.
4 Perform post-hoc media attribution via counterfactual masking (leave-one-out) and visual redundancy penalization without requiring explicit media-level conversion labels.
📊 Results
Evaluated on approximately 150M randomly sampled sessions, SMEO improves doubly robust CVR (DR-CVR) by 5.5% and reduces mean swipe count (MSC) by 15% over existing baselines.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Re-rank product media carousels (images, videos, 3D renders) on THE ICONIC product detail pages to surface conversion-critical assets earlier and reduce mobile swipe depth.
  • Use the leave-one-out media contribution and visual redundancy scoring to audit fashion catalogue quality and guide merchants on which media types (e.g., catwalk videos, size-spec images) to request or prioritize.
  • Deploy the offline batch inference pipeline to precompute optimized product media orderings in a key-value store for sub-5ms retrieval at request time.
