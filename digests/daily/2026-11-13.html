A Context-Aware Retrieval-Augmented Generation Framework for Enterprise Product Discovery Question Answering

10 Nov · Senior Technical Lead; HCL Canada

Product discovery in e-commerce requires efficient retrieval algorithms to answer user queries with contextually relevant items. This research introduces a Context-Aware Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework designed for enterprise product discovery and question answering using the Home Depot dataset. The approach combines domain-specific named entity recognition, Sentence-BERT embeddings, FAISS, BM25, and entity-based ranking fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion. It comparatively evaluates small language models like SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct and TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat-v1.0 for generation and factuality. The findings show that SmolLM2 achieved higher retrieval and generation scores, though statistical tests found no significant difference in overall system scores.

Key ideas
1 Combine domain-specific named entity recognition with hybrid retrieval methods like Sentence-BERT embeddings, FAISS, and BM25 keyword search.
2 Use Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) for entity-based ranking to enhance product matching accuracy.
3 Evaluate compact open-source language models such as SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct and TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat-v1.0 for e-commerce answer generation and factuality.
📊 Results
Tested on 35 queries, SmolLM2 achieved a retrieval quality score of 0.9328, a generation quality score of 0.7319, an overall score of 0.7342, a faithfulness score of 0.5870, and a hallucination rate of 0.4130.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Adopt the hybrid CA-RAG framework combining BM25, FAISS, and domain-specific NER to improve product search and conversational discovery for fashion queries.
  • Benchmark compact models like SmolLM2-1.7B for low-latency, cost-effective on-device or internal LLM applications.

Cyber Chaos Monkey: Staying ahead of security threats with AI agents

16h ago

Shopify developed an agentic vulnerability hunter called Cyber Chaos Monkey (CCM) that runs live against their production infrastructure to discover and fix security issues before attackers can exploit them. Conceptually, CCM is a graph-engineered bug-bounty researcher with access to source code, threat intelligence, and infrastructure records. Architecturally, the system separates concerns into a Discovery component for refining ideas and a Sandbox component for hands-on exploit testing. To maintain safety, Shopify implemented strict technical controls like containerization, least privilege, and egress proxies alongside alignment mechanisms like two-person integrity (TPI) using a separately aligned judge agent. So far, the agents have executed tens of thousands of offensive engagements and identified dozens of impactful findings without causing outages.

Key ideas
1 Deploy agentic vulnerability hunters that operate directly against production infrastructure using live test shops, leveraging an informational advantage over external attackers.
2 Separate the architecture into Discovery and Sandbox components, where Discovery refines hypotheses and Sandbox agents execute hands-on testing, fuzzing, and live API requests.
3 Enforce strict technical containment via containerization, least-privilege permissions, credential proxies, and an egress proxy operating on an allowlist within Kubernetes.
4 Implement two-person integrity (TPI) by running a separately aligned judge agent in a sidecar container that monitors executor session logs in real time and terminates the run if rules of engagement are violated.
📊 Results
The agents have executed tens of thousands of offensive engagements and identified and acted on dozens of impactful findings without causing an outage or incident.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Deploy autonomous agentic systems with strict guardrails and human-in-the-loop validation to continually stress-test internal search, recommendation, or LLM-based applications against edge cases and injection vulnerabilities.
  • Utilize two-person integrity (TPI) design patterns, where a secondary monitoring agent oversees a primary task-executing model in real-time, to govern automated personalization or marketing generation agents.

AI chatbots are recommending fake retail websites, study finds

14h ago

A recent study by IP protection firm Podqi tested ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity on shopping-related prompts for 151 brands to see if they recommend fake or risky retail websites. The research revealed that all three AI engines direct users to impersonators, knockoffs, reregistered domains, or dead sites during product discovery. Perplexity performed the worst with risky URLs in 15 percent of answers, while ChatGPT and Gemini showed them in 8 percent and 4 percent of answers respectively. The findings highlight significant risks for consumers and brands, particularly smaller brands which faced double the rate of risky site appearances compared to larger companies.

Key ideas
1 Audit RAG and citation pipelines to detect when LLMs surface reregistered, expired, or fraudulent domains during brand and product discovery.
2 Monitor chatbot outputs specifically for regional brand queries, which the study found triggered fake or risky regional sites more frequently.
3 Implement automated guardrails and warning systems similar to ChatGPT's approach, which flagged potential risks in a portion of its risky citations.
📊 Results
Across 6,795 answers tested by Podqi involving 151 brands, Perplexity showed risky sites in 15 percent of answers, ChatGPT in 8 percent, and Gemini in 4 percent.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC

LLMON: An LLM-Native Markup Language, now in Granite 4.1 3B and 8B

12h ago

LLMs often struggle with implicit structures and are vulnerable to prompt injections because formats like JSON are designed for parsers rather than causal models. To solve this, the authors developed LLMON, an LLM-native markup language that explicitly communicates semantics using reserved special-token delimiters and unique IDs to bind instructions to data. They released two experimental preview models, granite-4.1-3b-LLMON-preview and granite-4.1-8b-LLMON-preview, trained to consume and generate LLMON-marked text. The findings show that these models maintain general capabilities while drastically reducing indirect prompt injection success rates compared to standard models.

Key ideas
1 Use LLMON to explicitly separate instructions, data, and requirements using reserved special tokens and unique IDs, avoiding the ambiguity of JSON for causal transformers.
2 Bind specific instructions to exact data payloads via an execution block, preventing unrelated context passages, old chat turns, or tool outputs from hijacking the model.
3 Define custom domain-specific vocabularies and fine-tune a LoRA adapter on top of base LLMON models to handle structured agent planning trajectories like ScienceWorld.
4 Leverage provided lossless conversion tools to seamlessly translate existing JSON data pipelines into LLMON-formatted inputs.
📊 Results
On the OPI benchmark, LLMON avoids 47% more attacks relative to the best frontier model and 95% more than the best open-source model, nearly eliminating indirect prompt injection success rates while remaining on par with Granite 4.1 Instruct general capabilities.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Use LLMON for LLM-powered shopping agents and tool-calling flows to isolate user reviews or product descriptions from executing malicious hidden instructions.
  • Structure RAG contexts for product recommendations and size & fit queries using LLMON bindings so the model only references the exact retrieved documents bound to the prompt.

From prototype to production: Engineering agents with Agent DLC

15h ago

Building production-ready AI agents requires moving beyond traditional software testing to account for non-deterministic behavior, changing prompts or tool descriptions, and external API dependencies. The AWS Agent Development Lifecycle (Agent DLC) introduces a structured framework with an inner loop for iterative improvement against a fixed standard and an outer loop for updating standards based on production feedback. By defining criteria across five dimensions and three tiers, teams can systematically evaluate agent performance, diagnose failure paths, and make rigorous release decisions. This approach ensures that updates actually improve outcomes rather than masking deeper coverage or grounding issues.

Key ideas
1 Adopt the Agent DLC framework featuring two loops: an inner loop for rapidly improving agent performance against a fixed standard, and an outer loop for updating standards based on production feedback.
2 Evaluate agents using five key dimensions (e.g., quality, responsibility) and three tiers (e.g., hard constraints versus release gates), ensuring high scores in one area never offset hard-constraint violations.
3 Use multi-level path inspection (session, trajectory, step) to trace failures to their root causes before making isolated, single-variable changes to prompts or knowledge bases.
4 Establish a golden dataset of 20 to 30 approved examples with business owners within the first week to serve as the benchmark for evaluation and release decisions.
📊 Results
Chunk-level precision was 1.00 for all three tested questions, while fact-level precision dropped as low as 0.09, demonstrating that aggregate metrics can hide severe information coverage problems.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Build custom evaluation pipelines for e-commerce search, recommendation, and size-and-fit LLM agents using multi-dimensional criteria (such as fact-level source precision and recommendation relevance) rather than relying on aggregate metrics.
  • Implement an inner/outer loop engineering lifecycle to safely update customer-facing personalisation and chat agents without introducing regressions in product recommendations or violating customer data privacy constraints.
