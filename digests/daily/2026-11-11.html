Hybrid Product Search Without the Copy: Lexical, Semantic and Filtered Retrieval Served Directly from a Data Lake

21h ago

E-commerce catalogs are typically stored in lakehouse tables, but search applications traditionally require separate synced copies in lexical and vector engines. This paper introduces a lake-native search approach extending Apache Pinot to serve BM25 lexical scoring, approximate nearest-neighbor vector search, and reciprocal rank fusion directly over Apache Iceberg Parquet files. By adding a corpus-wide statistics protocol and leveraging file-level pruning, the system achieves exact retrieval quality without copying row data. Experiments show it becomes searchable seconds after a commit and sustains thousands of queries per second at scale.

Key ideas
1 Extend Apache Pinot with TEXT_SCORE to support BM25 scoring over Lucene text indexes without copying the underlying Parquet catalog rows.
2 Implement a two-phase corpus-wide statistics protocol (TEXT_SCORE_STATS) to ensure BM25 scores are globally comparable across separate Parquet files.
3 Execute hybrid search and Reciprocal Rank Fusion natively in standard SQL, pushing structured filters down to individual segment scans.
4 Use Iceberg snapshots and per-file indexes to achieve incremental freshness (searchable in seconds) without running expensive force-merges or sync pipelines.
📊 Results
On the Amazon ESCI benchmark (1.2M products), the lake-native hybrid search matches exact retrieval at nDCG@10 .361. On a brand-sorted lake of 277M products (0.8 TB), a single node answers hybrid searches in 6.5 to 9 ms (p50) and sustains 1,100 vector searches per second at under 180 ms p99.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Evaluate lake-native search architectures using Apache Pinot and Iceberg to eliminate redundant product catalog copies and separate sync pipelines for search and recommendations.
  • Adopt zero-copy remote index reading and global BM25 corpus statistics protocols to reduce infrastructure storage costs while maintaining ranking relevance.

Query- and Candidate-Aware Long User Sequence Modeling for Sponsored Product Ranking in Search

21h ago

This paper presents a production sequence framework for sponsored-product search ranking that extends recent user behavior by incorporating long-term history. The approach uses dual retrieval channels—candidate-nearest-neighbor and query-nearest-neighbor—to select relevant past actions from a lifelong action pool within latency limits. It encodes these selections alongside dedicated query and candidate context tokens, using candidate-aware early fusion to inject the candidate into every history token. Offline ablations show that combining dual retrieval with early fusion improves ranking metrics over the production baseline. In a live online A/B test, the model successfully increased click-through rate, conversion rate, and orders per impression.

Key ideas
1 Dual Contextual Retrieval: Augment recent behavior sequences by retrieving long-term actions using both candidate-to-item and query-to-item semantic similarity.
2 Candidate-Aware Early Fusion: Directly inject the candidate product representation into every history token to ensure explicit token-level compatibility modeling.
3 Hybrid Attention Mechanism: Use causal attention in initial transformer layers for history tokens alongside global context tokens, followed by fully bidirectional attention in later layers.
4 Budget-Constrained Sequence Design: Construct a 200-action sequence comprising 100 recent actions, 50 candidate-retrieved actions, and 50 query-retrieved actions to fit auction-time production latency limits.
📊 Results
In offline ablations on a held-out test set, the dual-retrieval model improved click AUC by 1.37% and order-7d AUC by 2.19% relative to the baseline. A five-week online A/B test on live sponsored-product search traffic showed increases in CTR, attributed conversion rate, and orders per impression.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Upgrade THE ICONIC's search ranking and sponsored products models by combining recent user click and purchase streams with query- and candidate-retrieved long-term history.
  • Implement candidate-aware early fusion in fashion recommendation transformers to explicitly model compatibility between candidate garments and historical user interactions.
  • Adopt hybrid attention patterns and auxiliary next-action prediction losses to better capture both immediate fashion search intent and long-term style preferences.

PailitaoMMSearch2.0 Technical Report: Scaling Generative Retrieval to Unified End-to-End Multimodal Search

27 Oct
Figure 1. PailitaoMMSearch2.0 overview. Data scaling expands supervision, intent scaling learns a unified policy, and scenario scaling exposes the learned capability under different resource envelopes.

Industrial generative retrieval models are typically specialized for fixed online scenarios and narrow query forms, lacking a unified approach for open-ended multimodal search. To address this, the authors present PailitaoMMSearch2.0, a native vision-language model system that directly maps raw query images and free-form instructions to product Semantic IDs without explicit intent routers. The system scales across data production through reusable toolsets, intent scaling via continual pre-training, multi-intent SFT, and Sample–Beam reinforcement learning, and scenario scaling using model capacity and reasoning budgets. Deployed on Taobao Pailitao, the model achieves substantial online business lifts, including transactions up by 27.04% and GMV up by 8.94%.

Key ideas
1 Formulate product retrieval as direct generation of three-level hierarchical Semantic IDs (8,192 entries per level) from a raw image and free-form text using a native VLM architecture.
2 Use a composable data toolset (coarse-filter, fine-filter, query-text-gen, CoT-gen, and quality-control) to automate cold-start synthesis and real-traffic refinement without manual end-to-end annotation.
3 Apply Sample–Beam rollouts during reinforcement learning, where reasoning prefixes are sampled while product SIDs are beam-decoded, better aligning training with online candidate-set generation.
4 Expose quality-cost operating points through explicit model capacity (0.8B, 2B, 4B parameters) and reasoning budgets (No-think, Low-think, High-think) without deploying separate intent routers.
📊 Results
In online A/B tests on Taobao Pailitao, PailitaoMMSearch2.0 increased item page views by 17.90%, transactions by 27.04%, and gross merchandise value (GMV) by 8.94%.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Replace or augment current multi-stage visual and text search pipelines with a unified multimodal generative retrieval model that maps outfit photos and chat instructions directly to product IDs.
  • Implement Sample–Beam reinforcement learning rollouts to optimize recommender and search ranking policies while aligning training behavior with inference-time beam decoding.
  • Adopt the composable data toolset approach to automatically clean user interaction logs, synthesize cold-start training data, and filter noise for new fashion categories or search intents.

garrytan/gbrain

5 Apr · ⭐ 32.4k (+549 today, +900 this week)

GBrain is a self-hostable, MIT-licensed memory layer designed to give AI agents persistent, structured, and controllable long-term memory. It stores explicit facts and pages in Markdown files backed by a database engine like PGLite or Postgres, exposing retrieval via MCP and a CLI. It combines keyword search, vector similarity, and typed graph traversals with an optional synthesis layer that answers complex queries with citations and gap analyses. The tool runs background enrichment and consolidation cycles to keep company or personal data fresh and up-to-date.

Key ideas
1 Use a dual-query design splitting raw hybrid search ('gbrain search') from synthesized, cited answer generation ('gbrain think') with explicit gap analyses.
2 Maintain markdown files as the source of truth backed by a portable database index (PGLite for local use, Postgres/pgvector for shared scale).
3 Implement bundled or custom schema packs with typed page taxonomies to scope expert routing, automated fact extraction, and path prefixes.
4 Expose memory securely over Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers, enabling multi-agent compatibility with tools like Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor.
📊 Results
On BrainBench relationship questions, relationship retrieval lifts production R@5 from 0.6626 to 0.7241.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Deploy a shared company brain via Postgres/pgvector to unify internal product documentation, customer insights, and engineering runbooks across data science and engineering teams.
  • Use typed schema packs and graph traversal to map product entities (e.g., categories, brands, styles, customer segments) for contextual personalization and search ranking agent workflows.
  • Integrate memory and MCP servers into internal developer tooling and coding assistants to maintain durable context across frequent model interactions and deployment cycles.

OneMall: One Architecture, More Scenarios — End-to-End Generative Recommender Family for E-commerce

3 Nov · 21 citations

OneMall is an end-to-end generative recommendation framework developed for Kuaishou's e-commerce services to unify multiple item distribution scenarios like product-cards, short-videos, and live-streaming. The approach mirrors the LLM pre-training and post-training pipeline by introducing an e-commerce semantic tokenizer, a Transformer-based architecture, and a reinforcement learning pipeline. The semantic tokenizer captures both real-world semantics and business-specific item relations, while the Transformer backbone uses Query-Former, Cross-Attention, and Sparse MoE for scalable auto-regressive generation. Furthermore, the reinforcement learning pipeline connects retrieval and ranking models, allowing the ranking model to act as a reward signal for policy retrieval optimization. Experiments show that this unified framework achieves substantial business and performance gains across multiple e-commerce scenarios.

Key ideas
1 Deploy an E-commerce Semantic Tokenizer to capture both real-world semantics and business-specific item relations across disparate presentation scenarios.
2 Utilize a Transformer-based architecture incorporating Query-Former for long sequence compression, Cross-Attention for multi-behavior sequence fusion, and Sparse MoE for scalable auto-regressive generation.
3 Connect retrieval and ranking models via a reinforcement learning pipeline where the ranking model serves as a reward signal for end-to-end policy retrieval model optimization.
📊 Results
Extensive experiments demonstrate that OneMall achieves consistent improvements across all e-commerce scenarios, including a +14.7% GMV increase in product scenarios.
🛍 How to apply at THE ICONIC
  • Unify THE ICONIC's multi-scenario recommendation touchpoints—such as product display cards, lookbook videos, and live-streaming sessions—using a shared generative recommendation framework.
  • Adopt the E-commerce Semantic Tokenizer approach to better capture fashion-specific semantics and cross-scenario item relations for apparel and lifestyle products.
  • Explore the reinforcement learning pipeline to connect retrieval and ranking models, using the ranking model as a reward signal to optimize policy-based retrieval for customer sessions.
